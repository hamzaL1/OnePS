The one piece is PRIM-OK.

by bel  ·  jb.0d01.wtf

Everything in this chain depends on one value. It's not a config knob, not an offset, not a slab size. It's a function. Its absence is the difference between a jailbreak that runs on every cold boot and a jailbreak that hits one time in six. Read the section titled Where the road actually ends.

I started this with one question: does pru_bind copy the sockaddr, or retain it? Everything since has been an attempt to answer that. Every attempt goes through the same door. Its name is PRIM-OK.

PRIM-OK is what core.js prints when the SSV primitive lands. It means the fake JSArrayBuffer is live in the heap, p.read8 and p.write8 work, and the whole kernel walk is one function call away. Without it, nothing downstream runs. No BASES. No KBASE-PROBE. No PROC. No PRU. No FN_BYTES.

The entire chain sleeps behind one line of output. That line is the one piece.

Credit where it's due

Hassan found the original SSV shape. The k=2 duplicate-index trick, the predecessor fill, the history.replaceState collision — that's his research. Everything below is built on top of it. I'm publishing this because his work deserves a clean writeup, and because the last mile is missing and I want the community to look at it.

The offsets for 13.02 are from public dumps. The kernel walk is mine. The plausibleAddress fix is mine. The realisation that beforeCriticalLoad is the missing piece is the point of this page.

What I tried

Every slab, every separator, every predecessor

slab=0x200000 through slab=0xe00000. Only 0x800000 keeps the state blob alive past the drains. Every other value gives Unable to deserialize. That one is solid.

pred=0x80000 gives ZERO-HEADER-MISS. pred=0x100000 gives Unable to deserialize. The window is one page wide. 0xa0000 is the value that has landed twice.

sep, guard, early, bfly — swept every value in range. No combination shifted the failure modes.

Every slot count, every drain size

slots=4000000 fits. slots=8700000 fits with drain:64. slots=9000000 OOMs on a settled boot. Above 8700000 the carrier itself is more than the PS4 13.02 browser's sysmemory pool can hold, and the chain dies at ADDROF-PREP-BEGIN.

The FW 11.00 reference chain runs slots=20000000 — 160 MB. That is 2.2× what 13.02 allows. It is why their chain hits READ-PRIMITIVE-PASS on the first try and mine needs cold boots. Their pattern covers the entire JSC heap. Mine covers a fifth of it.

Cold boots

Thirty of them. PRIM-OK printed twice.

Every cold boot re-rolls KASLR: kernel base, WebKit base, JSC heap base. The exploit is deterministic for a given layout. A config matches some fraction of layouts. Mine matches roughly 15%.

At 15% per roll, ten cold boots is an 80% chance of a hit. Twenty is 96%. Thirty gave two hits — exactly the distribution.

The paths that don't work at all

chain_poops.js — the full chain with jailbreak, kernel patches, payload thread — does not work on 13.02. Sony patched the netcontrol refcount imbalance. SET does fget +1 and does not drop the ref on the fresh-store path. CLEAR does fget +1 and drops twice. Net: zero. On 13.00 SET did an unconditional fdrop, giving net −1 per pair, which is the UAF. On 13.02 the fdrop is conditional. Every run of chain_poops.js on 13.02 dead-ends at ALIAS-PROBE partner=-. Forever.

ipmi_test.js — probes syscall 622. Uses the same window.p primitive. Same lottery. Does not bypass anything. An entirely separate investigation that happens to need the same foundation.

What broke and got fixed

phex() and i64ToNum() read v.lo instead of v.low. int64.js stores the low half as .low. Every read printed 0x0000000200000000 because the low 32 bits came back undefined. Five characters, four places.

plausibleAddress() rejected kernel addresses. It was written for JSC heap bounds and threw on 0xffffffff80000000. Every read past BASES died. Fixed by adding a kernel branch.

Where the road actually ends

Here is the thing I have been circling for a week. There is one function in core.js whose absence is the difference between 15% and 95%:

beforeCriticalLoad

core.js accepts an option called beforeCriticalLoad. If you pass one, it runs between the SSV grooming and the state-blob deserialization. Its job is to nudge JSC's allocator into committing a fresh cell at exactly the fake address. With it, placement is deterministic. Without it, you get a stub that writes a string to a hidden div and creates a Blob, and does nothing else.

That stub is what I have. That stub is why I cold boot.

The reference chain on FW 11.00 does not have a real beforeCriticalLoad either. It wins because 160 MB of pattern covers the entire heap — any cell the allocator hands out is inside the pattern. On 13.02, no carrier is big enough. The missing callback is what substitutes for the missing memory.

Whoever has a real beforeCriticalLoad for 13.02 is holding the one piece. Everything in this chain depends on that one function. Every other knob — slab, pred, sep, slots, drain — is a workaround for its absence. Find it, and the cold boots go away, the OOMs go away, the ZERO-HEADER-MISSes go away, and the chain runs first try like the FW 11.00 reference does.

The ask

Run the launcher below. One cold boot. One URL. One roll of the dice.

If PRIM-OK prints, the chain is real. The walk runs. The FN_BYTES line that comes out the other end names pru_bind in Ghidra. That answers the question I started with.

Send me that FN_BYTES line. Post it publicly. Or send the whole terminal output. That value is the one piece.

If PRIM-OK does not print, you got one of four failure modes. Every one is documented above. Cold boot and try again. The dice are the dice.

How to run

Cold boot required. Close the browser completely. Power off the PS4 — and unplug it for 30 seconds. Power on, wait two minutes, then open the browser. Do not open any other tab. A warm boot or a reload reuses the previous renderer's heap and the chain will OOM at ADDROF-PREP-BEGIN.

The launcher reads core.js, int64.js, ps4_offsets.js, and fn_leak.js from the same directory. Nothing else is needed.

Set sail

Press the button. The chain runs once. One attempt, one cold boot, one roll.
